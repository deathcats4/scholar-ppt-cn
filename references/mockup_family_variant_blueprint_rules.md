# Mockup Family and Variant Rules

Create a family blueprint only when it improves reusable visual planning.

Useful cases include:

- long decks;
- repeated evidence types;
- several related result pages;
- explicit sample-first requests;
- expansion from approved visual directions.

Skip it for simple planning, one-off diagrams, or local slide revisions when it
adds no value. Record the reason in `workflow.steps`.

When used, record:

- family ID, name, purpose, visual identity, hierarchy, evidence behavior,
  density range, annotation language, variation strategy, and slide IDs;
- variant ID, family ID, name, candidate composition behavior, adaptable
  regions, applicable asset geometries, and slide IDs;
- the selected family/variant and decision reason on each mapped slide;
- representative sample slide IDs when visual samples are planned.

Use a family to coordinate the deck's identity and cross-slide rhythm. Do not
define a family as one repeated page skeleton. A variant is a candidate response
to a communication task, not a box layout that content must fill.

Define enough variation to avoid repetition; do not enforce a fixed minimum
when the deck does not need it. Recompose a mapped slide when its real title,
evidence geometry, density, or neighboring-slide rhythm makes the recorded
variant unsuitable. Preserve the reason for the change in project state.
